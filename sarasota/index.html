Preventing Water Damage in Sarasota Homes

Houses tell you when water is getting where it should not. The signs are easy to miss if you do not know them, so here is what our technicians look for in Sarasota homes. Paint that bubbles or peels, a brown ring on a ceiling, baseboards pulling away from the wall, and flooring that cups or lifts all mean moisture behind the surface. A musty smell in a closet, a toilet that rocks, or a tap that drips belongs on the list too.

None of these is an emergency on its own, but each is cheaper to fix now than later. Re-caulk tubs and showers when the bead cracks, since water behind tile does damage you will not see for months. If you have a sump pump, pour a bucket of water into the pit a few times a year to confirm it switches on. In basements and garages, shelving and plastic tubs keep belongings clear of the floor.

No amount of maintenance prevents every leak. Fewer and smaller leaks are still a real gain, especially compared with a Sarasota home full of drying machines for most of a week.

Common Water Damage Issues in Sarasota

Some water problems need a phone call this minute, and others can safely wait until morning. We have ordered these five common Sarasota calls by how fast each one needs attention.

Bursts: A pipe that has split is the most urgent call there is, because it puts out water for as long as the supply is on. Cold weather, depending on the season, is one trigger, with the leak appearing as the frozen section thaws. Close the main valve, open a faucet to relieve pressure and call right away, even at night. If a ceiling is sagging from the weight of water, stay out from under it and do not poke it.

Runoff: Rain running into the house at ground level ranks a close second. There is no valve to close here, so lift what you can off the floor and avoid wet electrical outlets. This water has crossed lawns, driveways and streets, which makes it unsanitary. As soon as it is safe to enter, we pump the water out and take away porous items it ruined.

Kitchens: A dishwasher that leaks from its door or a refrigerator line that drips behind the unit calls for same-day attention. Turning off the supply valve for that appliance normally ends the leak itself. What remains is the water trapped below the cabinets and floor covering, and it will not dry by itself. In Sarasota kitchens we often dry cabinets in place by sending air through small holes in the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Moisture under a raised floor is seldom an emergency, but it should not be put off for a season. Puddles on the ground cover, dripping ductwork or wet insulation between the joists are what an inspection usually turns up. Left alone, the damp air rises into the rooms above and the wood framing begins to decay. Our crew clears any standing water, brings the framing down to a normal moisture level and reports the likely cause.

Odors: Last on the list is a stale, earthy odor in a room that once had a leak, which is worth a scheduled visit. Since growth can start 24 to 48 hours after materials get wet, an old leak that was never fully dried is a likely culprit. Do not scrub or spray a large patch yourself, because disturbing it spreads spores through the house. Our technicians test the area, contain it and remove the growth along with the moisture feeding it across Sarasota.
