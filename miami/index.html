How Miami Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

Water damage looks sudden, but the causes are usually slow: a hose that aged, a seal that dried out, a drain that clogged a little more each month. Knowing the usual suspects is most of the prevention for a Miami homeowner.

Pipes and fittings top the list. Watch your water bill and your water pressure, because an unexplained rise in either can point to a hidden leak or stress on the pipes. Next come appliances. Run the dishwasher and the washer only while someone is in the house, and change their hoses before they show cracks. The roof and the ground outside are third. Missing shingles, cracked flashing, blocked gutters and soil that slopes toward the house all let rain find a way in.

Leaving the house empty for a while? Close the main valve before you go, or have a neighbor look in. Davie Restoration is glad to talk through prevention with any homeowner in Miami. And if the worst happens anyway, a fast call keeps a small leak from turning into a large repair.

What to Do Before Our Miami Crew Arrives

People mean well in the first hour after a leak, and some of what they do makes things worse. Here are the slip-ups we run into most on Miami jobs, with the better option for each.

Do not use a household vacuum on water. It is an electric shock risk and it will ruin the machine; use towels and a mop. Do not turn on ceiling fans or lights under a wet ceiling, and stay out of rooms where the ceiling is sagging. Do not lift tacked-down carpet yourself, since it can shrink or tear and may be saveable if it is handled properly. Do not turn the heat up high to dry things out, because warm, damp air is what mold likes.

The helpful things are simple: close the valve, prop open cupboards and wardrobes, and stand wet cushions and textiles up to drain. Photograph each room before you shift a single item. Do keep children and pets away from the area. And do make the call early, so Davie Restoration can have a crew on the way to Miami while you do the rest.

Restoration Challenges Miami Homeowners Face

Water damage does not always arrive as a sudden gush across the kitchen. Below are the situations our crews are called to most often around Miami, and what each one tends to involve.

Hidden leaks: A pinhole in a supply line or a loose drain fitting can drip inside a wall for months. Clues include flooring that feels spongy, blistered paint and a higher water bill you cannot explain. Our technicians trace the wet area with moisture meters and thermal cameras, without tearing out more wall than needed. Left alone, constant dampness rots framing and feeds mold, so a minor drip is still worth fixing now.

Sewage: A blocked sewer line sends waste back into the house, usually at the lowest shower, toilet or floor drain. It is typically caused by roots in the line, built-up grease or a municipal sewer overwhelmed during a downpour. Sewage counts as Category 3, the most contaminated class, and it is not safe to clean up bare-handed. Carpet, padding and other absorbent materials that got wet usually cannot be saved.

Storms: During a heavy storm, runoff may collect along the foundation faster than drains can carry it off. From there it comes in under doors, through window wells and across garage floors. Floodwater from outside picks up soil, fertilizer and whatever else it crossed, so we treat it as contaminated. A big enough storm can cause it at any time of year, and Miami properties are no exception.

Crawlspace: The space beneath a raised floor is easy to forget, which lets dampness go unchecked for years. Standing water, a torn vapor barrier or a leaking pipe overhead keeps the air below your floors humid. You may notice cupped floorboards, a stale smell in the living area or insulation hanging loose. We pump out what has collected, dry the joists and subfloor, and show you where the moisture is coming from.

Condensate: An air conditioner pulls gallons of water out of the air on a humid day. That water should leave through a small drain line, which clogs easily with algae and dust. A blockage makes the drip pan spill over, wetting the ceiling or floor near the indoor unit. Most owners learn of it from a ceiling stain or drooping drywall, so have the drain line cleared once a year.
